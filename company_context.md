# inDrive company context for media intelligence

inDrive is a global mobility and urban services platform. The core business includes ride-hailing/taxi services and can also include adjacent services such as delivery, courier, freight, and city-to-city mobility depending on market availability.

Important product audiences:
- Riders/passengers: price transparency, safety, wait time, availability, trust, payment, support, trip quality.
- Drivers: earnings, commission, fuel costs, onboarding, verification, safety, demand, incentives, fairness, support.
- Couriers and delivery users: delivery reliability, availability, price, SLA, courier experience, safety, support.
- Local operations teams: regulation, licensing, market launches, supply/demand balance, incidents, reputation, partnerships.

Important product areas:
- Pricing and fare negotiation: impact on conversion, acceptance, affordability, driver earnings, market competitiveness.
- Safety: rider safety, driver safety, incident response, verification, emergency tools, trust signals.
- Driver supply: onboarding, retention, earnings, fuel costs, commission, incentives, protests or strikes.
- Passenger demand: availability, reliability, price perception, app-store ranking, local brand trust.
- Regulation and compliance: licenses, bans, permits, local transport rules, legal status, government relations.
- Delivery/courier/freight: reliability, courier supply, delivery pricing, service quality, merchant/customer experience.
- Competition: Uber, Bolt, Grab, Gojek, DiDi, Careem, Yandex Go and other local ride-hailing or delivery platforms.
- Reputation: incidents, public safety stories, press coverage, partnerships, awards, app rankings.

What a product manager needs from each article:
- The essence of the article in Russian.
- How exactly inDrive is mentioned: product, market, regulation, safety, drivers, passengers, delivery, competitors, reputation.
- Why this matters for product or operations decisions.
- A concrete implication, not a generic statement. Prefer "check onboarding", "review pricing/commission", "assess safety flow", "monitor regulation", "compare competitor feature", or "validate market communication".

Do not overstate conclusions. If the article title/snippet is thin, say that the signal is based on the available headline/snippet.
